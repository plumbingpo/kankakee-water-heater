With an electric water heater, the symptom is often a strong clue. No hot water anywhere suggests a tripped breaker, a tripped high-limit reset, or a failed upper element or upper thermostat, because the upper side controls when the lower side gets power. A few minutes of hot water followed by lukewarm points to the lower element or lower thermostat. Water that comes out scalding points to a thermostat stuck closed or an element shorted to the tank, which is also what trips the red reset button. Those are the calls we answer most in Kankakee, IL, and nearly all of them are fixable.

Kankakee Water Heater tests electric heaters with a meter, not a hunch. We look for scorched wires, melted insulation, and loose terminal screws, since a loose connection can heat up enough to damage the thermostat and the wiring around it. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • The water heater breaker tripping over and over
  • Rust-colored hot water or moisture around the heater
  • A shower that turns lukewarm much sooner than it used to
  • Popping, sizzling, or crackling sounds from the tank
  • No hot water at all from any faucet
  • Tap water much hotter than the thermostat setting
  • Scorch marks, melted insulation, or a hot smell at the wiring
  • A red reset button that keeps tripping

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Flush the tank regularly to keep minerals from piling up around the lower element
  • Get a diagnosis for any reset button that trips more than once
  • Test the temperature and pressure relief valve once a year
  • Replace a worn anode rod before corrosion moves on to the tank itself
  • Keep the thermostats set around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Never turn the breaker back on until the tank is completely full of water
  • Protect your elements from hard water with a softener or upgraded low-watt-density elements
