How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Distant fixtures that take minutes to run warm
  • Energy costs climbing while usage stays flat
  • Unsteady delivery temperatures that suggest a failing mixing valve
  • Rusty water or damp spots around the equipment
  • The tank giving up in the middle of a rush
  • Trouble reaching sanitation temperatures in the kitchen
  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • A heater old enough that a failure feels like a matter of time

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Replace consumed anode rods before the tank itself starts to corrode
  • Budget for replacement as equipment nears the end of its expected life
  • Flush commercial tanks more often than residential ones, often every few months in heavy use or hard water
  • Leave space around commercial heaters for airflow and safe servicing
  • Record fixture temperatures regularly, especially before inspections
  • Confirm the recirculation loop still delivers hot water quickly to the far fixtures
  • Test thermostatic mixing valves periodically to confirm safe delivery temperatures
