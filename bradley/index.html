Keeping Your Bradley Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Bradley Homeowners Face

Around Bradley, water heater trouble tends to come from a handful of sources. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, hard water may be a real factor: dissolved minerals drop out as sediment on the tank floor, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the rumbling and popping you may hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and that's why annual descaling is worth keeping up with in hard-water areas.

We also see a lot of aging equipment. A standard tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod is often used up, leaving the tank open to rust. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and heating elements and thermostats on electric ones.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well may see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, which a different anode rod often cures. A tankless heater in hard water should get regular descaling. And an older install getting a new heater often needs code items that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a correctly piped relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. Every one of these problems can be fixed, and most are much cheaper to address early.
