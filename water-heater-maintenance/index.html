A water heater has two slow enemies: sediment and corrosion. Sediment comes from minerals that separate out as water is heated and sink to the bottom of the tank, where they cut efficiency, cause popping and rumbling on gas models, and overheat the steel or bury the lower element on electric ones. Corrosion is held back by the anode rod, a sacrificial rod of magnesium or aluminum that wears away so the tank lining is spared. Nobody sees either process happening, and by the time a homeowner notices, the damage is usually done. That is the reason maintenance matters for Kankakee, IL homes: it interrupts both problems while they are still cheap to fix.

A Kankakee Water Heater visit is designed to stop both of those processes early. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, remove and check the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we check element resistance and thermostat operation and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, verify the temperature setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

Tankless systems are included. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that restrict flow and reduce efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and look at the error history for patterns. Whatever type you have, you receive a plain summary of the unit's condition and any parts approaching the end of their life. To schedule a visit in Kankakee, call (815) 205-4629.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact Kankakee Water Heater at (815) 205-4629 for a prompt inspection.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
