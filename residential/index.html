No Surprises

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Kankakee Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing for every home, covering everything from a burned-out heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment settles on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod quietly dissolves, and the steel underneath starts to rust from the inside with no outward warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, or moisture around the base, schedule an inspection sooner rather than later. An early diagnosis often keeps a repairable issue from becoming a replacement.

How old is your water heater? The date code on the label usually gives it away. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one forces the issue. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Preventive Care

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Each maintenance visit from Kankakee Water Heater is more than a quick drain and refill. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, lifts and tests the relief valve, checks the burner, pilot, and venting on gas heaters, tests the elements and thermostats on electric units, and confirms a safe water temperature. Small problems get caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and the heater stays running at full efficiency.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, makes the burner work harder, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your heater's age, your household's hot water demands, and your local water.

The savings show up quickly. A tank without a sediment crust heats water with less effort, a replaced anode rod can add years to the tank, and a tested relief valve means the tank can safely vent pressure if something goes wrong. Next to the cost of replacing a tank early, a maintenance plan is a bargain.

Proper Sizing

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

An undersized water heater runs out before everyone has showered, while an oversized one wastes energy keeping gallons you never use hot around the clock. Kankakee Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute to the fixtures you run at once and the temperature of your incoming water.

Picking a new water heater is about more than tank size. Our technician will compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, laying out operating costs, venting and gas line work, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, and a properly sized tank is often the simpler, lower-cost option. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Kankakee

A cold tap where hot water should be, a pilot that won't stay lit, a tank full of sediment, a slow drip from the relief valve: each of these problems has its own cause and needs its own repair. Kankakee Water Heater tracks down the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's an electric tank with a burned-out element, a gas heater with a failing thermocouple, a broken dip tube sending cold water straight to the tap, or a tankless unit throwing error codes.

The correct fix depends entirely on what actually failed. On electric tanks, it's usually an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure problems are solved with a thermal expansion tank plus a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before the damage spreads. We also check the temperature setting, since water above 120°F can scald kids and older family members in seconds. A tank leaking from the steel itself can't be patched, and we'll tell you so. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
