Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Replacement?

Consider the vent first. Gas water heater exhaust carries carbon monoxide, so an undersized, badly sloped, or loose vent can spill it back into living space. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and the details pile up quickly. Many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code, and some jurisdictions require a permit. Switching to tankless or a heat pump adds new gas line sizing, vent materials, circuits, and condensate lines to the list.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
